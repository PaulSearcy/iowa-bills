# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Iowa residents looking up how a legislator voted on the floor. They arrive with a name, a bill, or a news story in mind — not with a spreadsheet. Secondary users include journalists and advocates who need the same record, faster and denser.

Typical context: a kitchen-table or phone session after a headline, a caucus email, or a conversation about “what did my representative actually do.” They are not legislative staff. They should not need to know chamber procedure to get an answer.

## Product Purpose

Make Iowa House and Senate floor votes readable. A visitor can pick a session, switch between a comparison table and a bill-by-bill list, filter by chamber, and see who voted yea, nay, or was absent.

Success is three jobs on one surface:
- Find a bill or member quickly
- Compare members across many votes in one table
- Leave able to explain the vote to someone else

This is a civic record, not a campaign and not a replacement for legis.iowa.gov’s official documents. Links back to the source stay available.

## Brand Personality

Warm, inviting, explainer. Civic education first; density when the visitor asks for it. Voice is plain Iowa English — direct, unhurried, never cute and never partisan. The interface should feel like a patient person sitting with you over the roll call, not a portal or a scorecard.

## Anti-references

- **legis.iowa.gov** — dated chrome, cluttered search, hard-to-scan roll calls. This tool exists because that site is the official source and a poor reading experience.
- Do not inherit its visual noise, mystery acronyms as the only labels, or “you already know the process” information architecture.

## Design Principles

- **Civic first, tool second.** The table and list serve a person trying to understand a vote, not an analyst trying to feel powerful.
- **Show the roll call, don’t decorate it.** Vote state (yea / nay / absent) is the information. Party and chamber are context, not branding.
- **Two ways in, one record.** Table is comparison; list is explanation. Session, chamber, and bill identity stay consistent across both.
- **Uncluttered on purpose.** Every control earns its place by helping find, compare, or explain. If legis.iowa.gov would add it, question it.
- **Public default.** An Iowan with a name and a bill number should succeed without knowing House vs Senate procedure.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 AA is the public default: keyboard access, visible focus, labeled controls, sufficient contrast, and `prefers-reduced-motion`. Color must not be the only vote signal. Party colors are informational, not a substitute for text. The vote table is dense by nature; it still needs a readable alternative (the bill list) and headers that make sense to a screen reader.
