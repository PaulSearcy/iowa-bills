How the totals are built

Candidate spending is total disbursements by the nominee's principal campaign committee for the 2025–2026 cycle, primary and general combined. Hinson's Senate committee is her former U.S. House committee, so only activity since Jan 1, 2025 is counted; Turek's committee began reporting Jul 1, 2025. Raised includes transfers from joint fundraising committees, which FEC lists separately. Outside money is independent expenditures reported on FEC Schedule E: spending that supports a nominee or opposes their opponent counts for that nominee's side. Coordinated spending by the national and state parties is not included, nor is spending by groups that is not reported as an independent expenditure (such as issue ads).

Outside spending covers the whole cycle, so some of it was spent during the primaries. Not counted for either side: $232,500 by Iowa Action supporting Zach Wahls in the Democratic primary, and $15,476 by Indivisible Action opposing Joni Ernst before she left the race. Candidate totals stop at the pre-general reports covering through Oct 14; the last three weeks appear in the post-general reports, due Dec 3, 2026. Independent expenditures are reported within 24–48 hours, so those run closer to Election Day.

How the results are measured

The winning margin is the winner's votes minus the runner-up's votes. Points are that margin as a share of all votes cast in the race, write-ins included. A candidate who lost shows how far behind the winner they finished. Money per vote is the side's total money (campaign + outside groups) divided by the votes that nominee received.

Sage, Carlin, Norris and Scholten did not reach the general-election ballot, so they have no general-election votes.
